Forward Sprinter Van Build Progress

12.12.2015byJustin Fricke//Leave a Comment

With wires hanging and vapor barrier flapping in the wind, the van's been looking like a scene out of Dexter and has really been screwing with Adam's OCD. He was the happiest between the two of us to finally be able to get "walls" up and making everything look more together.

Up first was hanging the angle aluminum to the back of the van. We chose angle aluminum since it's lighter than steel or iron and is easier to work with. We were afraid it might bend, but that was a risk we were willing to take and we had a solution in mind in case that did end up happening. We drilled holes into the angle aluminum, got it level in the van, drilled holes into the walls of the van, and then used pop rivets to hang the angle aluminum. For a couple of guys who've never used a pop rivet gun in their lives, we're happy with how it turned out.

—–

Before we could start hanging our walls we had to affix furring strips along the walls of the van. Usually, furring strips are hung horizontal and are used to hang cabinets. A van's a little different so we hung our furring strips vertically in order to put up the walls. These strips can also be used as studs to hang cabinets.

We started off by figuring out where we should attach the 1×2 furring strips to the walls of our van (had we needed to insulate the van, we'd be doing it right now, but ours is already insulated, thus saving us a step). Since the van's bowed we divided the walls into three sections: top, middle, and bottom. To give us the proper drilling points, we spaced them out according to the ribs along the van walls. The spacing's uneven, but we can easily find our furring strips by finding the heads of the screws. In order to drill into the metal walls we used 1 & 1/4 inch #8 self-tapping screws. Lots of them.

—–

The walls were pretty easy to hang. We cut the 1/16″ inch Luan Plywood to size and lined them up with the furring strips and used the same screws we used to affix the furring strips to the van walls.

—–

Our most daunting task was to make cabinets to hang above the bed platform, where we'd be sleeping. The last thing either one of us wants is to have a cabinet come crashing down on us in the middle of the night and wake us from our beauty sleep. Sprinter Van Diaries gave me a great idea to use 3/4″ inch Birch Veneer Plywood as the sides and to use the van's walls and ceiling as the interior of the cabinet while drilling the cabinets into the walls and ceilings to hang. They also gave me the idea to cut out a hunk of cardboard that matches the curvature of the van and use that to draw templates of each end of the cabinets.

We traced that stencil onto the plywood and started hacking. After the 2×2 furring strips were measured, we put them into position, 2 across the top and 3 across the bottom. Once we had them secured with the 3 1/2″ #8 wood screws the real test came. Adam held up one cabinet as I guided into place and started attaching it to the walls. I started with two screws through the 2×2 and into the ceiling furring strip followed by three screws to the top back and bottom back 2×2 and finished it out with two more screws to the top 2×2. After stepping back we took a deep breath and looked at what we had just done. I grabbed the cabinet and shook it softly, then violently, so the van started rocking side-to-side. That thing's not going anywhere!

That same process was repeated for the other side and we now have two cabinets hanging above the future bed platform. Adam was thoroughly shocked that the whole idea resulted in sturdy cabinets. I'm happy we have a place to store clothes that are easily accessible.

For shits and giggles we even put a level between the two and wouldn't you know it, they're level! A van's completely different than a house and they're not going to always be level since our parking spots may not always be level, but just let us have this win.

Custom fit pieces of 1/16 inch Birch Veneer Plywood was then secured to the bottom 2×2 furring strips. More of the 3/4 inch Birch Veneer Plywood was used to make the cabinet doors (a total of 4). Sliding doors would've been ideal, but we managed to make functional cabinets and we didn't want to test our luck. Simple door knobs were attached and we used cabinet catches to keep those doors from opening while we're driving. So far so good.

—–

Laying 3/4″ Pine Plywood from edge to edge of the angle aluminum would surely put too much stress on the plywood, and possibly the aluminum. We decided that if we wanted to have a chance at having a bed platform without any support underneath that would take up valuable storage space, we'd need reinforcements. After we cut down nine 1×4's to size, they were slid into place with ease leaving only the plywood to lay atop. Surprisingly enough our idea worked and there's only a slight flex in the platform when the two of us are on it. Some rigidity, in form of a foldout table, was added to the front where most of the weight will be while sleeping. It had to be closed off and we used some of the leftover 3/4 inch Pine Plywood to cover most of it. There's a gaping hole in the center which is where we put our collapsible table.

—–

Our table is going to be used a ton for working, eating, and just hanging out. It also serves as a door that'll be locked down when we're not around, or en route. Some of the leftover 3/4 inch Birch Veneer Plywood was cut to size, sanded, and water sealed. It's attached by a couple of hinges and stands up after a couple legs fold down. When it's time to sleep or leave for the day it'll be locked down after being latched into place.

—–

That brings us to our seats. Bench seats were the way to go for a couple of reasons. Each one can comfortably sit two and we can store things inside, and secure what's inside, with a latch and lock. Our original plan was to build a back rest that would transition to the wall, but once we put the first bench seat in place we realized the wall would do just fine as a back rest.

—–

At this point we're in a holding pattern. We've done all we can with the inside of the van build and we're really proud of what we've accomplished so far. When our solar panels arrive early next week we'll be able to get them mounted to the roof and run the wires down the inside of the wall. When that happens we'll be able to install our second bench, build a kitchen counter, and one last overhead cabinet above that counter and when that's all said and done, dare I say it, we'll be done with the van build!
