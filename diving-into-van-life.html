Diving Into Van Life

01.12.2016byJustin Fricke & Adam Fricke//8 Comments

No amount of research or planning can substitute for first-hand experience. This is why you learn to swim in the pool, not from the top step. We worried so much about preparing and leaving, that we didn’t give much thought to the trials and tribulations that we might face while adapting to a new lifestyle.

We didn’t dip our toes in. Nope, we climbed straight up to the high dive, threw our towel aside, and did the damn thing. After a week of treading water, here’s what we’ve learned:

We Annoy Each Other

We’re brothers and we’re expected to fight. Cram two brothers into 82 square feet and all hell’s liable to break loose. It has a couple times. If you have siblings, think of the things they do that annoy you and multiply that by at least 10. That’s what it’s like living in a van with your sibling, with their annoying habits at an arm’s length.

Justin:

I knew Adam and I vent our frustrations differently when something doesn’t go as planned, but I never realized how different. I get super mellow, shrug it off, and find a solution. On the other end of the spectrum is Adam who goes all aggro for a short few seconds and is then done.

I’m always the first one up. It’s always been that way and it’s a drag getting the kid out of bed in the morning.

Maybe my hearing’s going bad or maybe my mom was onto something when she asked Adam to speak up. He’ll throw out an idea and I won’t hear him for the life of me. I’ll come up with that same idea later and he’ll go off on me. I’m convinced he mumbles.

Adam:

I know Justin was getting over a cold recently so I didn’t mind if he made that disgusting hacking sound as he cleared his sinuses. But I’m pretty sure that’s gone on for too long and it’s getting old. Gross.

When I think I have a good idea, I ponder it many times before voicing it. So when I voice it I expect it to be heard and taken into consideration. Justin does this thing where he shoots down my bright ideas and then comes up with the same solution days later and claims them as his own. I don’t know if he can’t hear me or if he doesn’t pick up what I’m laying down.

Sometimes he tries to be my parent, now that we’re away from our real ones. I understand that he’s the big brother but if he were doing this trip with a friend of only 2 years age difference, he would see them as an equal. Is it so much to ask for the same respect?

Expectations vs. Reality

Things are not what they appear.

Justin:

Rainbows and unicorns is what I expected. Leaving home was supposed to be much easier, we were supposed to wake up to a beautiful sunrise in a stunning place every morning, and finding wifi should be easy. That’s how it was supposed to go, but it’s been quite the opposite. Last week you saw me lose it when I left home, our primary parking location at night has been a Walmart parking lot, and we quickly found that Starbucks has the only reliable wireless connection.

Adam:

I pictured the two of us always being upbeat, meeting tons of like-minded people, and having plenty of free time to find quiet time, master crafts, or begin learning new crafts. In reality, we’re always agitated with each other since we are always together. Still, nobody understands what the hell we’re doing. And our days are often filled with every day tasks such as trying not to starve to death and organizing the living space, leaving little time to explore, be alone, or master new skills.

Looking Forward

The road is long and winding but the future looks bright…or maybe that’s just the sun setting on the horizon. Either way, it’s pretty.

Justin:

The first 4 days were the hardest for me. I hate change and I’ve had to adjust to living in close quarters, transition from having a solid schedule to making a new schedule every day, and feeling like we’re doing nothing meaningful. I was a jerk those first 4 days. I wasn’t having any fun, but everything’s started to change. After a phone call with my best friend, I’ve realized I really do enjoy sleeping in Walmart parking lots and that even though I’m not “inspired” how I thought I would be, I need to find those stories I was seeking. They’re there, I just wasn’t turning over every stone.

Adam:

Adjusting to this new way of life has been anything but easy. It sounds glamorous and may even look that way through our photos and videos. If that’s the case, we’re doing a good job at fooling you. But this lifestyle is far from glamorous. It took me a full 7 days to wrap my head around that concept. But once I understood it, accepted it, and embraced it, I began enjoying the daily struggles. I’m 23 years old and I get to do what many people can only dream of doing. I’m done moping around, wishing that this would feel like a vacation. I’m going to breathe in the freezing cold winds with a smile, choke down our tasteless dinners with gratitude, and continue jumping the hurdles that this journey throws at us. This is just the beginning and I can’t wait to see how far it takes us.

—

Now that we’ve taken the plunge into the deep end, we’ve finally started to swim. It wasn’t easy at first, as you can tell, but it’s all worth it. We still love each other and things are starting to fall into place. If there’s anything we’ve learned so far, it’s that you need to understand who you’re going through a period of life with.

Living together for 23 years, you’d think we knew each other really well. That’s a big fat lie. After only a week on the road, we’re just now breaking the surface and really getting to know each other, understanding what makes the other tick and what we can do to help one another along the way when we fall short.

1 week down. 51 more to go.
