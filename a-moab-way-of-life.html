A Moab Way of Life

04.26.2016/By Justin Fricke/The Road

Photo · BLM camping in a Sprinter Van outside of Moab, UT

We came roaring into Moab, UT off Highway 191 in the late morning. I hadn't showered in a few days and forgot to brush my teeth that morning when we pulled over to cook breakfast at a truck stop off I70. I smelled gross and felt sorry for anyone who had to come within a five foot radius of me. As I drove us down Main Street to the Visitor Center I realized my stench might actually be acceptable as I passed by all the DIY campervans, trucks with pop tops, cars that people are living in, and mass amounts of vehicles with bikes hung all over them.

After getting some outdoor ideas at the visitor center we went over to the local climbing shop where the guys working sold me on the guidebook, a solid purchase. One of them had just bought a Sprinter and we shared our build out knowledge, or lack thereof, with him and checked out the handy work he'd accomplished in his so far.

A friend of mine told me about the Moab Cyclery that lets anyone and everyone fill up their water tanks from a hose outside their store. We decided to fill up before heading out to find some free camping in the desert. As we pulled up and moved things around to get the fresh water tank out a guy walked up to us. "I like your van," he said. He was walking to his van parked a few spots over with extension chords running out the back to the shop's outdoor electrical sockets. He's a bike guide at the shop, from Ohio, and we talked about van life and he told us some spots to hit when we're in Michigan, even though he's from Ohio.

Photo · Mesa Arch in Canyonlands National Park

The whole reason we came to Moab was to film some videos with our friends, Chris and Lauren from Sierra Trading Post, for their YouTube Channel. But that wasn't happening until later in the week and we had a few days to kill. Picture this: wake up in the desert where you camped for free just a couple hundred yards away from people that paid a couple hundred dollars for one night to glamp, take some photos of the sunrise, climb in the morning, take a dip/bath in the river to cool off in the afternoon, and climb some more with the potential for a skate sesh.

That was our daily itinerary in Moab.

The past few years I'd been fantasizing about this lifestyle; I'd get all my work done for the week in one or two days and play all week. It'd be my way of being a dirtbag and my time in Moab made that dream become a reality.

Photo · Adam Fricke bouldering in Moab, UT

Everywhere we go it's like we have to explain ourselves and what we're doing. But in Moab it's like everyone gets you. Someone starts talking to you about your van and then they tell you about the trip they're on, are planning, or just got back from. Everyone loves the outdoors and has somehow found a way to structure their lives around being outside as much as possible.

Photo · Camping out on BLM land outside of Moab, UT

It's been a week and Moab's feeling like home to me. Like the start of our journey, it's time to leave home once again because there's so much more to see, do, and experience this year.
