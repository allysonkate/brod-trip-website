Floating by Bismarck, North Dakota

By Adam Fricke • September 27, 2016

I could be wrong, but I don’t think North Dakota is seen to many as an outdoor adventure destination. I sure never saw it that way. But fortunately, North Dakota Tourism showed us the error in our ways. They even offered to give us the grand tour of their fine city. And what better way to get a lay of the land than by floating through the area?

We put our kayaks in at a boat ramp and stepped into them carefully. After we had gotten our sea legs under us, we cruised downstream at a consistent 7mph. It was a crisp, sunny, early autumn Monday morning without another soul on the water. Usually it’s known to be busy with pontoon passerby, but this day it was just us and our new friend Mike.

Mike was our “guide” for our paddle down the Missouri River. As an employee for ND Tourism, he was kind enough to skip out on a day of meetings to help us get our boats in the water, navigate the waterway, and brief us on the rich history of Bismarck. As a local native, we couldn’t have asked for a more knowledgeable guide. And the fact that Mike made for good company on the water was just an added bonus.

From the get-go, we were immersed in history, like the Northern Pacific Railroad Bridge. It was completed in 1882 in efforts to more efficiently ship agriculture and consumer goods into the area. While the cage of the bridge has been replaced over the years, the hand-laid stone pillars remain of the original structure. That’s right, 100-year-old stone pillars support the crossing trains with ease!

As we paddled and drifted at a steady pace, we took time to stop occasionally to gawk at gulls and Canadian Geese honking at each other on sandbars. As the river is always changing, so are the sandbars and wildlife that inhabit the area. North Dakota is also known for seeing the fall foliage before anybody else. Even though we were there early in the season, the bank was littered with colorful trees, making our morning on the river that much brighter.

One of the most impressive historical landmarks we’ve seen this year was Fort Abraham Lincoln. Sitting on the bank of the Missouri River amongst foliage and blue skies, the facilities of Fort Lincoln demands attention of passerby, especially while on the water.

Who knew what interesting history the state of North Dakota holds?
