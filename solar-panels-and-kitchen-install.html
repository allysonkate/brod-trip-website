Solar Panels and Kitchen Install

12.28.2015byJustin Fricke//1 Comment

Who would've known that art class would help give us the knowledge needed to install solar panels atop our van? We didn't, and we'd like to thank all our art teachers in school for teaching us how to use tape and glue.

Our Commandr 20 solar panels are flexible and easy to fold. They're lightweight and easy to carry, but we need them to stay in one place. After a full day, literally, of "dynamic" discussions, we decided the best way to mount them to the roof would be with tape and glue (heavy-duty adhesive and caulking). We were thinking about building a frame for them, but we realized it wouldn't really add any rigidity to them and the chance of them flapping like crazy as we drive would be high.

I got up on the roof in the blazing hot Florida winter sun to begin prepping the surface. First I took a good ol' bucket of soap and water and got to scrubbing. Then I took a little acetone to the dried area to remove any grit and grime that the soap and water left behind. Adam and I used VHB 4950 tape by 3M. We attached strips to the underside of the solar panels and then unrolled them, laying them into place. Last on the agenda was to use some seam sealer to fill up all the empty spaces between the solar panels and the roof. We don't want water getting stuck underneath there. That wouldn't be good for the solar panels or the roof of the van.

When the solar panels were mounted and the glue dried, we turned our attention to the interior. Once the wires to the solar panels were run down to the inside of the van to connect with our Generatr 1200 we were able to install the final bench seat. For our kitchen cabinet we took a stencil we had made for our overhead bed cabinets. Once the birch veneer was traced, we cut it out along with more 2×2's and got to attaching everything together, like we had done before.

The installation was easy since we had done it twice before, and then we started scoping out our floor plan for the kitchen countertop. After a lot of deliberation we had our dimensions in place and a rough idea as to how to build it. We'd attach 2×4's with corner brackets (not angle brackets) to make a "U" shape. Those would be the main supports and they were attached to the floor using corner brackets as well. We took measurements and cut out 3/4″ birch veneer and then waterproofed it to use as the countertop. While that was drying we took two pieces of pine plywood and cut them to size to serve as center dividers, and extra support. By the time we cut them to size and installed them, our countertop was done drying.

Finally we installed our countertop, after cutting out holes for the sink and pump faucet. Angle brackets hold the countertop to the 2×4's and pine plywood dividers/supports. To our surprise, it's a pretty darn solid countertop!

With a kitchen cabinet and counter in place we finally get to call it a day! The van build is done, sort of. There are two more shelves we've already cut out that need to get installed inside the countertop. That'll happen this week when we figure how much space we want/need for dishes and cookware. It needs to happen fast since we set out on the open road on Friday!
