JOURNEY TO ALASKA | 5 DAYS ON THE $115 MILLION HIGHWAY

07.28.2016 • Adam Fricke

It probably comes as no surprise that Alaska has been high on our list of states we’re most excited to visit. Perhaps even #1! We’ve gotten lots of questions about how we’re covering Alaska and one of the most frequently asked questions is, “How are you getting there?”

We just rolled into The Last Frontier and it was a long journey. We took a legendary stretch of road that begins in Canada and ends here in Alaska. It’s cleverly named The Alaska Highway.

It was built to transport goods needed for WWII from Alaska to the contiguous United States. When completed in 1942, it measured at 1,700 miles long and took just over $115 Million to build. Over the decades, improvements have been made to stretches of the road to make it more accessible. Although improved since its original construction, it’s by no means a walk in the park. Each winter takes a toll on the asphalt and upkeep is a constant necessity for it to remain passable.

We’ve heard rumors about treacherous roads, wildlife encounters, and mechanical breakdowns. We’ve also heard that it’s been modernized and paved to the point that “it’s not that shit anymore.” We couldn’t decide what to believe so we figured the only way to know for sure is to go. So that’s what we did.

The Alaska Highway is seen to many as the ultimate road trip. I can attest that it totally is. It tests the strength of your vehicle, the strength of your will, and the strength of your relationship with the person you deem co-pilot. We spent 5 days on the highway testing all of those things.

Expecting to spend 3–4 solid driving days to get from Squamish, B.C. to Fairbanks, AK, Justin and I casually left on a Monday afternoon to tackle the legendary stretch of concrete and dirt. Before officially getting onto the Alcan, we had to spend some quality time with Highway 97, one of the roads that connects to the Alaska Highway in Dawson Creek. After 6 hours of listening to the first Harry Potter audiobook, we found ourselves coasting into a familiar place. Walmart. We cooked a quick dinner and hit the hay to the comforting sound of shopping carts clanging outside our windows.

Day 2 brought us officially onto the Alaska Highway. After hearing so much about it, I began worrying that it would be nothing but smooth sailing. I got exactly what I was hoping for on Day 3. Not a grizzly bear fight. But definitely some wild times… After 6 hours of the second Harry Potter audiobook, we got our first real taste of the Alaska Highway. We came across Muncho Lake. I couldn’t resist a bit of self-induced mild hypothermia. Justin took the wheel for a bit after my toes thawed out and I changed clothes.

Less than an hour later we drove right by a family of bison, complete with adorable calf. We stopped to join in on the family time while keeping a safe distance. Just a few miles further and our jaws dropped. The rest of the bison herd was enjoying their evening right next to the road. Even on the road! After weaving our way through, we pulled off and cooked dinner just before the sun started going down at 11:00PM.

Day 4 started late. After enjoying our riverside camp spot, we enjoyed a little jaunt down the river and got back into the van to hit the road. By mid-afternoon we found ourselves in the Yukon Territory. We stopped at Signpost Forest, a popular roadside attraction, to stretch our legs and get some info. The lady at the visitor center informed us that the next stretch of the road would take an extra 2 days. We were determined to prove her wrong — mainly because we were getting stir crazy and wanted to spend some time on the Alaskan trails already!

With the Chamber of Secrets in our rearview mirror, Day 5 was spent on a notoriously rough section of the highway, weaving through potholes and hurdling ice heaves. Slowed only by the occasional construction area, we cruised right over the border and through Border Patrol without a hitch. We made it to Alaska.

Although we were pretty nervous to take on the challenge, we’ve found there isn’t much we can’t overcome. By preparing the best we can, facing obstacles head on, and taking the time to put one foot in front of the other, we can do just about anything. Whether it’s building out the van with no experience, pushing ourselves to the limit, or covering 1,700 rugged miles in just 5 days, we’re capable of so much more than we give ourselves credit for.

I’m guessing you are too.
