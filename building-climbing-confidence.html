Building Climbing Confidence

01.21.2016byJustin Fricke//2 Comments

After two weeks of the coast and waves (which we both love) we finally set our sights towards the mountains. You know what that means? Climbing! I’m the climber between the two of us and feel the most comfortable on the sharp end of the rope. Knowing that we’re going to get to some awesome rope climbing destinations out west, Adam and I needed to brush up on our rope climbing skills and build trust as climbing partners.

My friend told me about this climbing crag, Rocky Face Mountain Recreational Area. After taking a look at some online photos and reading up on the place, I knew this would be the perfect place for our first climbs of the trip. There’s always an approach to the climbing area, but not here. Park your car in the parking lot and walk along a sidewalk to the climbs. This kept our spirits high while getting back into the swing of things. We also needed to dry out our wetsuits and we could keep an eye on them the whole time.

We kept it mellow, only top roping the first day and sticking with climbs under 5.8. No need to show off; we both wanted to build up the belayer–climber trust with each other. Later that night we posted up at another Walmart. This Walmart was different as it was right across the street from a juvenile detention center. Oddly enough, I felt fairly safe as I went to sleep that night.

We woke up the next morning, drove back to Rocky Face and made Spam and eggs. This day we focused on sport climbing. Adam had never done a sport climb and the kid needed to get good at clipping the rope to quickdraws and feel comfortable taking a little whipper, should he fall. We thought we’d take it easy and only do climbs under 5.8. That’s how it started off. But Adam felt comfortable later in the day to be the rope gun. He took the first lead, placing the quickdraws all the way up the route. He did great and it was so rad seeing him get the little climbing bug we all get when we have our first fun day.

Some local climbers we met talked me into a 5.10 just a couple routes over. I was hesitant, but after working the moves out in my head from the ground, I was feeling it. As I started up and made it past the first two bolts, I was feeling confident in Adam as my belayer. He fed me the right amount of rope I needed as I climbed and never short roped me when I went to clip the rope.

I found the upper crux of the route and calmed myself as I figured out my next move. After some contemplating and deep breathing I went for the hold I had my eye on, but I couldn’t figure out what to do from there. “Falling!” is what I yelled as I lost my grip and took a little whipper. Adam caught me with ease and even said he had some fun getting dragged up the rock face a little bit. Once we had a little laugh I hiked back up the rope to the last bolt I passed and started reworking the route in my head. Eventually I got past the crux — one fall was all it took — and I clipped the anchors. It was so much fun and Adam lowered me feeling confident in my rope climbing ability once again.

We left it set as a top rope for Adam to cruise before finishing out the day on a simple 5.7, which Adam had a blast setting. As the sun began to set, we headed out of the park feeling confident as climbers and each other’s belay partner.
