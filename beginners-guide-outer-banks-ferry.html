Beginner’s Guide to Using the Outer Banks Ferry System

01.16.2016byJustin Fricke//2 Comments

Expected file: 160108_OuterBanks-035-1.jpg

When we were initially looking at going to the Outer Banks, we planned to follow Google Maps. That’d take us across a couple big bridges to the center of the Outer Banks. It’d be at least a six hour drive and let’s face it, just another drive. But after a Facebook conversation with our aunt, we set our sights on taking a ferry.

After a fun surf in Myrtle Beach, SC, Adam and I posted up in a Starbucks to grab some wifi. He worked on videos and I worked on learning how the Outer Banks ferry system works. Turns out it’s pretty simple and these ferries run often, even during the off-season. Within 45 minutes I was comfortable that I knew what ferries we needed to take to get to where we were going and bought the ticket we needed to start our journey.

Here’s What You Need to Know About the Outer Banks Ferry System

Figure Out Where You’re Going

Without a clue as to where you’re going in the Outer Banks, I can’t help you. Once you do have an idea as to your starting point and your end point, go to the NCDOT ferry website to see all the ferry terminals and get familiar with where they are in relation to your start and end point.

Pick a Time

The ferries are on a rigid time schedule and you need to plan accordingly. Some routes have a ferry leaving every hour on the hour, while others have only a couple ferry runs a day. Take a look at the schedule and make sure you’re reading the correct departure time for the correct terminal. It’s easy to get confused, don’t let it happen to you or your whole day might be screwed. Also make sure you give yourself plenty of time to get from one ferry terminal to the next if you have to take multiple ferries.

Make a Reservation

Certain ferry routes allow you to make a reservation online. Since it’s their off-season, we would’ve been fine without a reservation. If you’re going during peak season, you’ll definitely need to make a reservation in order to get the ferry you want. Some ferry routes are on a first come first serve basis — for that, get in line and pick a good radio station because you could be there for a while.

On the Ferry

You’ve finally made it, you’re on the ferry! If you drove your car onto the ferry, you’re sitting in your car getting transported on a boat and that’s rad! Take advantage of the passenger lounge upstairs. It’s warm in the winter and a great place to meet the other passengers. Alternatively you could hide out in your car: eat, sleep, do whatever you want. Enjoy the ride!

In Action

This is the route Adam and I took, to put everything into perspective for you.

We were coming from Myrtle Beach. The Cedar Island terminal was the closest. We needed to be at Kitty Hawk on Saturday to pick up a package from the post office and we were traveling on a Friday. We decided on the 7:00am time slot to Ocracoke to ensure we’d get on an early ferry from Ocracoke to Cape Hatteras.

By 6:30am we were staged and ready to board. Right before 7:00am the workers pulled us and two other cars onto the ferry and we were off! Once the ferry was underway we hopped out of the van and walked around the deck. With the wind and the ferry cruising at a solid 18 knots, the wind was whipping! We headed up to the passenger lounge and hung out with some other dudes that were going surfing to pass the time.

On schedule at 9:15am, we were driving off the ferry onto Ocracoke. Knowing that we wouldn’t make the 9:30am ferry to Cape Hatteras, 12 miles away, we took our time. Pulling off at some day parking areas to check the waves, grab some photos, skate a little, and see our new friends catch some waves.

We took the 10:30am ferry from Ocracoke to Cape Hatteras and stayed in the van. The ferry was much smaller, still windy and cold, and the passenger lounge was much smaller. Hot oatmeal in the van sounded like a better plan. Like clockwork, we rolled off the ferry and onto Cape Hatteras at 11:30am.

The Outer Banks ferry system is one of the easiest ways to navigate the islands. Don’t let it scare you. Take your time, do your research and you’ll be fine to sit back and enjoy the ride.
