Mountain Biking the Maah Daah Hey Trail

By Justin Fricke • September 30, 2016

My stomach churned as I rolled my bikes into storage in December. My road and mountain bike gave me so much joy and freedom. Between the two, I probably rode 1,000 miles across Florida’s flat roads and sandy trails in a year. Between what we were already bringing, bikes just didn’t make the cut and I had to be fine with the realization that I wouldn’t do much bike riding, if any, this year.

On our last day in North Dakota I finally got to jump on a mountain bike. I’ve heard the Maah Daah Hey Trail is pretty unreal with its steep drop offs and fluid downhill sections that’ll potentially send you over the handlebars. I was stoked to rent a full suspension bike from Dakota Cyclery and get some local knowledge of the trail: where to go, how far to go, and what to expect. Adam on the other hand was a little nervous.

The wind smacked us on our first uphill stretch right away. It was a cloudy day, but the wind and constant shade kept us nice and cool. That was a plus. The first bit of our time on the trail was fairly easy with short low-angle climbs followed by some easy downhill sections. I took the lead eventually as Adam was still trying to figure out when was the right and wrong time to shift into a different gear.

As we kept following the trail it progressively got harder and harder. We found ourselves slamming on our brakes to get through the tight switchback turns so our momentum wouldn’t send us flying over the edge of the tall butte and we occasionally had to hop off our bikes when the trail got too steep.

When we got past marker number 41 we made one last hill climb. Jennifer at Dakota Cyclery told us that spot would be a great turnaround spot as the trail would get even more strenuous and we’d be in for a lot more pedaling than we had anticipated if we ventured further. As the wind was howling over the butte we were standing atop, Adam and I thought back to a New York Times article we read about the Maah Daah Hey Trail. The author and some friends tackled the 96 miles of the Maah Daah Hey Trail on a multi-day bikepacking trip. That means they had to tackle the same trail challenges we faced, but with gear to keep them going for the day until they arrived at their predetermined campsite to resupply for the night and the next day. Needless to say that we were quite happy only having to worry about our daypacks filled with just enough water and minimal camera gear.

Jennifer had told us we’d do most of the work on our ride out and our ride back would consist of us cruising long, flowing, hills and turns most of the way back. As we kicked off we were having the time of our lives pumping through all the sections she had described and found ourselves cheesin’ ear to ear as we flew along the trail.

As I came flying back into Medora and back to Dakota Cyclery, legs burning, and my rear a little saddle sore, I couldn’t help but think about how much fun it’d be to do a multi-day bikepacking trip on the Maah Daah Hey Trail. Throughout the year I’ve been making mental notes of places I’d love to come back and visit and bikepacking the Maah Daah Hey Trail surely made its way to the top of this list. Who knew there was such amazing mountain biking in North Dakota?
