Embracing My Inner Tourist | Hueco Tanks State Park

03.18.2016byAdam Fricke//Leave a Comment

While Justin was attempting his Birthday Challenge, I was busy filming the video. Little did I know that filming a climbing video would entail climbing myself. That’s right, even “hiking” to the climbs in Hueco Tanks State Park had me putting on my climbing shoes and chalking up my hands. While everyone else was comfortably mountain-goating up the boulders, I was taking extra precautions. And that’s just to get to the climbs!

I’m not the strongest climber, I will openly admit that any day of the week, including holidays. But it is in my competitive, manly-man nature to want to be good at things. So I’ve taken it upon myself to use this year on the road as an opportunity to get better at things that I’m not good at. So far, my cooking skills have gotten noticeably better and I’ve grown comfortable parking the monster that is our home. But climbing is something that’s going to take some work for me.

Although I’m fairly athletic, I’m by no means overwhelmingly tall, strong or flexible. All of which are great assets to have in climbing. I also lack patience but I’m working on that, too. Let’s face it, learning something new is hard. When you learn something new you’re really bad at it. And being bad at stuff is not fun. It sucks.

But I read a really useful piece of advice in a great read called Do Over by Jon Acuff. This great piece of advice? Be a tourist. It sounds absolutely ridiculous but it’s a ridiculously useful mindset to have. When you’re a tourist, you’re going somewhere new. Uncharted territory. Because it’s new, you’re not expected to know the ins and outs like a local. You don’t know, and you can’t be expected to know. You’re a tourist!

So what do tourists do that make them so great? They ask questions. They try things they’ve never tried. They take stupid selfies of their stupid faces using stupid sticks because tourists don’t give a shit what they look like. They’re there to have a good time, and to ask you all the annoying questions you could possibly think of. Tourists have fun without shame and they learn more, faster.

So as I continue my climbing (but mostly falling) journey, I’m striving to have a tourist’s mindset. I’m asking questions about the routes, techniques, and lingo. I’m caring less about how clumsy I look falling off a warm-up route. I’m not scared of being scared as I clamber up an approach on my hands and knees, just trying not to die. But the important thing is that I’m learning quickly and I’m having fun in the process.

I don’t look like a professional because I’m just starting. I’m new. So shut up and let me take a stupid selfie of my stupid face on a stupid stick. I’m a tourist.

What skill could you be learning faster with a tourist’s mindset?
