The Ridgway Way

06.14.2016/By Adam Fricke/The Road

Justin has a lot of "internet friends."

Over the past 4 years of running his own outdoor blog, he's made acquaintances on the web that he'd never met in person. Since being on the road we've been able to meet some of them. Strangely enough, they all turn out to be pretty damn cool. Such was the case with Beth and her dog, Sprocket.

Before visiting Colorado, I knew Beth as "the blogger girl with the black lab." Over the course of 3 days, Beth turned out to be much more than that. As a high school science teacher, she has summers off. Instead of drinking margaritas on a beach, she's spending the summer working multiple jobs. She owns a sweet piece of property in Ridgway, CO and is saving to build her house on the property.

Rolling in late one night, we parked in her yard, shared some beers and laughs, and called it a night. Later the next day, in between her 2 shifts at work, we met her and her pup, Sprocket, for pizza at the local hangout. We met some of the locals, shared travel stories, and had a laugh at Justin for being the only person there who didn't like Gary Clark Jr.'s music. Just like that, we'd been accepted into the inner circle of their tightly knit town.

When Beth went back to work, we took Sprocket to the reservoir. I've never seen a dog so in love with the water. Justin and I traded off throwing a stick for him. At first he leapt off a rock to chase after it. After an hour or so of this game, his leaps turned into trots. Nevertheless, any time we threw a stick he'd go out and retrieve it. If we didn't put the leash back on him to bring him back home he probably would've stayed in the water all day.

Apparently Sprocket rests quickly because the next day he was kicking our butts on a hike all the way up to Twin Peaks. Beth and Sprocket led the way, only stopping because Justin and I were out of breath every quarter mile or so. Although it was a rather strenuous trail with a lot of elevation gain, Beth and Sprocket didn't seem phased. It was just another hike.

If it weren't for Beth, we probably wouldn't have spent much time in the little town of Ridgway. But it seems to be a trend that the places I'd never heard of before end up being the most memorable. Between the great people, authentic food, and panoramic views, I could definitely get used to living the Ridgway way.
