Pic(k) of the Week — #4

Wet Elk

11.18.2015 by Justin Fricke // 1 Comment

We took a little time off from the van build and went to North Carolina for a few days with our parents. It's possibly going to be our last family vacation before we set off on our Bro'd Trip on January 1st. It was hard to trade sunny skies and perfect van building weather for drizzly, dreary skies in North Carolina, but everything was booked and we made the best of it.

Our family that lives in North Carolina gave us a tip that we'd be there during the time that an elk herd makes its semi-annual pilgrimage to Cataloochee Valley in the Great Smoky Mountains National Park to graze on the tasty, grassy fields. With other plans already set in stone we headed out in hopes of seeing the elk herd on a Saturday, which just so happened to be the rainiest day of our trip.

As we rolled through the park gates we realized that we scored BIG. Usually the elk heard only comes out to graze at sunrise and sunset. They're also in the back of the park in a field that's far, far away. Within a couple minutes of driving through the park we found them in a field off the side of the road. There were probably 20 or so scattered throughout the field. Some were laying down, some ate grass, and others just stood there. Then there was one bull elk.

This bull elk was clearly the dominant male of the herd, or trying to establish his dominance. He would come close to me while I took his picture. He'd then walk over to another bull elk in the field and lock antlers with it. It's their mating season and this one bull elk in particular wanted to find himself a fine female to mate with.

I got this shot of him and the real story is what was going on behind the scenes. It was a strong drizzle and a camera + rain = death to camera. My camera and lens were good for a little while, but after 5 minutes Adam and I both had water collecting on our cameras. Our mom came to the rescue when she busted out an umbrella from the car. She held the umbrella over Adam and his camera while he took pictures. When my camera and lens started getting saturated, she'd walk over and hold the umbrella over me, leaving Adam and his camera and lens to get wet.

Lots of people would hide in their car, but the rain actually helped us out in a few ways: hardly anyone else was there, the rain on the elk's fur gave it more contrast and helped bring the photo to life, elk sometimes come out earlier when it rains so we didn't have to wait so long and had better light to shoot, and it was fun to problem-solve on the fly.
