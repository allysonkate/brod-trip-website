Hiking On Water To Shoshone Falls

June 28, 2016  •  Justin Fricke

Waking up and getting moving in the van is tough. The sleep's usually mediocre at best and we're always tired. So you can imagine how sluggish we are getting moving when we get to sleep in a real bed, especially at a place like the Blue Lakes Inn in Twin Falls, ID. Breakfast was calling downstairs and the coffee, eggs, bacon, cereal, and cinnamon buns made it slightly easier to get up and moving. Once breakfast was done we made the short drive down to the Snake River.

You know when you do something you love over and over again, it kind of gets old, but you still love it? That's how hiking's become for us, especially spending so much time in the desert. So when we got our kayaks from AWOL Adventure Sports, we were pretty stoked to be exploring a desert canyon from a new perspective.

The morning was tough, paddling under the Perrine Bridge against the wind and current. What should've taken us maybe an hour to get to Pillar Falls, took us double the time. We carried the kayaks over the first waterfall and were greeted with a calmer current and lighter winds as we kept paddling towards Shoshone Falls.

We heard the roar of the waterfall that's bigger than Niagara Falls before we rounded the bend in the Snake River. As we approached Shoshone Falls the waves grew bigger. The size of the waves under the falls showed off how massive Shoshone Falls really is.

A little rock shelf gave us what we needed to keep our gear dry, letting us paddle closer to Shoshone Falls. It's crazy being able to paddle so close to Shoshone Falls, the water cascading over the 212 foot falls causing big waves at the base. Adam and I paddled as close as we safely could one at a time, Adam paddled closer, while the other shot photos from the rock shelf.

The paddle back was easy. Just a slow drift down the river as the current carried us and we snapped photos we hadn't been able to get before. We talked it over and agreed that kayaking gave us a new way to explore. It's kind of like hiking on water!

Original photo unavailable — lost in the archive

Expected file: 160620_Idaho_TwinFalls-0229-1.jpg

As we drifted closer to Centennial Park to return the kayaks, we saw BASE jumpers hucking themselves off the Perrine Bridge. The Perrine Bridge is the only manmade structure in the U.S. that's legal to BASE jump off. Having seen BASE jumpers jump off that bridge so many times in various videos, I was stoked to actually see people doing it in real life.

Our bodies were aching after 8 hours on the water. After dinner, a quick soak in the Jacuzzi at the Blue Lakes Inn was just what the road doctor ordered. It was after 9:00pm as the sun set on the first day of summer. All we could do was crash hard to get ready for another big day in Idaho.
