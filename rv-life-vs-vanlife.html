RV Life vs Van Life

July 5, 2016  •  Justin Fricke

Now that we’ve lived both RV Life and VanLife, we can firmly say that Van Life is the way to go for us. Adam and I had been living the RV Life for a month while we waited for our van to get repaired. We’ve caught some flack in the comment sections of previous posts saying a “cheap RV would’ve been better” or that “an RV can go anywhere a van can go.” Now we can totally debunk any myths and give our comprehensive breakdown of RV Life vs Van Life.

RV Life

Pros

  • Onboard bathroom
  • Ample seating for you and guests
  • Roomy refrigerator
  • Lots of sleeping space
  • Ability to plug into power at RV sites

Cons

  • Gas mileage is terrible
  • Storage space is not efficient
  • Have to buy propane to keep the refrigerator running
  • RV sites cost a pretty penny and in order to power a lot of appliances you have to be plugged in
  • Generator is loud and runs a lot of the onboard appliances when not plugged in
  • The clearance is terrible making offroad travel near impossible
  • Dumping human waste is disgusting
  • It’s almost too comfortable and wants to keep you indoors
  • It’s like driving a tank

Van Life

Pros

  • Gas mileage is decent
  • You built the interior so you can take pride in what you built
  • The build out is customized to your specific needs
  • It’s cozy and a great place to come back to after a fun day outside
  • Most have great clearance
  • Most have an external battery bank to charge things
  • It makes you use less and be more conscious of electricity and water usage
  • Much easier to drive

Cons

  • Limited seating
  • Limited sleeping space
  • Your build out isn’t as sturdy
  • You can only plug into an RV outlet if you cut into your van and install a plug
  • Refrigeration turns into a challenge with the limited space
  • You might have to take a squat next to a bear to do your business

There are lots of pros and cons to each, as you can see. I think the biggest difference that Adam and I have found is that RV Life is much more comfortable than Van Life. Comfort on the road may sound ideal, but it can also be a hazard.

You see, it was hot in Kanab, UT. We were staying at an RV park and with Rosie The RV plugged in so we could run the onboard a/c unit to stay cool inside. That kept us inside while it was uncomfortable outside. Had we been rolling in Jolene, it would’ve forced us to think outside the box and find a more fun alternative: a swimming hole, for instance.

Plain and simple, RV Life has made us soft and more appreciative to the luxuries of living in a house. We live in a van and enjoy living the Van Life. It might be less comfortable than an RV, but Jolene kicks us out every morning when we wake up and forces us to have an incredible time outside and welcomes us back every night with a nice place to cook and catch some sleep.

Justin Fricke — July 8, 2016

Thanks for your insights, Bill. We have looked at an RV built on a Sprinter chassis and still found the cons to outweigh the pros for us. For a couple guys in their 20’s, we’re totally fine using public restrooms and going days, maybe even a week or two, without a shower. We also value storage space for our gear that gives us a monthly income over refrigerator, bathroom, and liveable space. The extra width is also a big hindrance because we do like to go down narrow dirt/gravel roads to find neat trails, climbs, and free camping. Above all, when we were in an RV, it kept us inside more.
