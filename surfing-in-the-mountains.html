Surfing In the Mountains

06.09.2016/By Adam Fricke/The Road

Durango, Colorado never struck me as much of a surf destination. But while in the Utah desert, we caught wind of a river rapid wave. Desperate to get in the water, the little mountain town of Durango skyrocketed to the top of my list of places I wanted to surf (Fiji, Australia, and Indonesia are also on that list).

There's something about being in the desert, away from the ocean, that makes a crumbly, 3-foot, chocolate milk colored river wave sound oddly appetizing. We bolted straight to the river's edge to check out the spot and saw a handful of river rats ripping on the standing wave. They made it look pretty easy!

Instead of ocean waves moving from offshore and crashing onto a beach, river waves work exactly the opposite. The wave faces upstream as the water rushes toward the surfer. In the ocean you surf with the wave and in a river you surf against it. The way the rapid stays still amongst the raging river creates a totally different surfing experience. In fact, it's nothing like surfing in the ocean.

This being said, it should come as no surprise that I didn't catch on as quickly as I would've liked. After many failed attempts at simply catching the wave, I began blaming it on the equipment. Clearly, the board I was riding was an ocean surfboard, not a high-volume river surfboard that others were using. The helmet was fairly comfortable, but the bulky life jacket we rented was making me slip off the board. After sliding off a half dozen times, I was close to ditching the life jacket. But being swept downstream and given a nice hold-down in the freezing cold rapids, only to be brought back up by the floaty foam, made me realize how important that piece of equipment is. I learned that I would just have to learn to deal with it and work around it.

After a few different attempts, trial and error (and the nice guys that actually know how to river surf) taught me that starting in front of the wave was not the right way to paddle into it. Instead, you have to start behind the wave, off to the side, and let a little current jet you right into the wave from behind!

Once I finally got the hang of catching the wave, standing up and riding it was the next challenge. I felt most confident with this aspect of it, but as it turns out, riding the wave is just as humbling as trying to figure out how to get into it. I got up maybe 3 times and struggled to figure out how to stay balanced, let alone turn, across water hurdling at me at 4,000cfs (cubic feet/second).

On my last attempt, I was swept all the way downstream. I hung on to Justin's surfboard for dear life. I went over the next set of rapids and the board got hung up on a rock, screwing up the fin box. Once I was no longer on the board, I kept going down the rapid floating on my back, as you're supposed to. What they don't tell you is that when you float over a rapid on your back, you're sure to smack your ass on the biggest rock in the river. That's gonna leave a mark!

River surfing isn't one of those things that I'll figure out right away. I've accepted that. But a broken fin box (sorry Justin) and a bruised bum won't hold me down forever. I'm determined to redeem myself another day, on another river. To be continued!
