Bagging Sends at Rumbling Bald

02.02.2016byJustin Fricke//0 Comments

After our day of hiking with Katie at DuPont State Forest, we weren’t about to take it easy. We woke to a frozen water line for our sink, thanks to the balmy teen temps that night. Rather than cooking eggs and oatmeal, we settled for ham and cheese sandwiches for breakfast.

We let it warm up a little before setting out and met up with Katie at the Rumbling Bald State Park parking lot for an afternoon of bouldering. The Bald holds a little piece of my heart since it’s the first place I went for outdoor climbs, and I was psyched to get back and, hopefully, put a couple projects to bed.

After the 15 minute uphill hike, the blood was coursing through my veins and I was ready to climb. The Trailside Boulder was our first stop. It’s my favorite warm-up boulder with tall V1’s with lots of holds and different variations of climbing it. After that I took a stab at an unknown V3 with a fun little sit start. Katie gave it a couple hoopy-de-hoops and Adam got his first dose of bouldering. The kind where you know you can send the problem, but for some frustrating reason your body just won’t let you.

I tried an awesome route called Red Sleigh Down a couple years ago when I was out climbing alone and stumbled across some other climbers just enjoying themselves. Needless to say that it’s one of the toughest boulders to find and I had no idea how to get back to the boulder, it’d been like 2 years for crying out loud!

Eventually Katie found it and we took a couple stabs at the boulder problem. If you ever get the chance, give this thing a try. It’s got jugs, crimps, slopers, a knee bar, heel hooks, and toe hooks all in one! Everything you could ever want in bouldering in one boulder problem. And none of us could bag the send.

Adam and I were reminded that people have “real” jobs when Katie had to peace out a little early to get some work done. Next up was the Shady Grove Boulder because I needed to take down Basketball Mantle. Not only does it have a sweet name, but there’s also a gnarly top out that’d been killing me the last two times I was there.

This time it took me one go. I won’t go into detail about the Florida Flounder I exhibited as I shimmied myself onto the boulder, but I got the send. That’s the funny thing about climbing; we’ll try a route over and over and over again and we just get stuck. We come back the next day, weeks, or years later and we get the send on the first go!

Last up was a fun little V3 with a big throw to a jug I came across in the guidebook. The sun was setting as we hiked back and we were greeted by a couple State Park Rangers. They were cool, one of them just wanted to hangout and talk about our van set up all evening, but they had State Park Ranger duties to attend to. We on the other hand, had a hot date with some BBQ back towards Asheville.
