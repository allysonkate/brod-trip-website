Nashville…It’s More Than Music City

From // Nashville…It’s More Than Music City

I’ve never really enjoyed cities. To me they’re just a bunch of buildings and traps to get you to spend money. As we headed to Nashville I wasn’t expecting much. After four days, I was blown away. Nashville put things into perspective for me. A city isn’t made by the buildings and the corporate bars and restaurants there. The people who live there are what give the city its vibe and character. There’s a lot of character in Nashville and what I think I loved the most about it is that everyone seems to be working towards something and is always willing to help one another.

My best friend, Tanner, just moved to Nashville. He knew it was time for a change from the bitter coldness of northern Wisconsin, so he took a risk and moved down there, found a place to crash for a month, and started looking for a place to make some money while he hustles his spoken word poetry project. After a week of his moving down, we rolled into town and he let us stay with him the whole time we were there, no questions asked.

Right when we got into Nashville we met up with our friend Ben. He’s lived in Nashville since going to college at Belmont and owns the stage when his band, My Red and Blue, is playing a gig. He welcomed us to his city and showed us around before he had to go to work. The next day he took us over to the HQ of his fiancée’s jewelry company. When we got there, Emily gave us the grand tour of where she and her team make beautiful custom jewelry.

For the longest time I thought you had to go to school to learn something specific, work for someone else, and then maybe get to live your dream by starting your own company. Emily shattered that whole preconceived notion of mine when she started Consider The Wldflwrs, a dream of hers. She had a dream, honed her craft on her own, decided to take a risk and not let fear get in the way by starting her own jewelry company — and now she’s doing pretty good for herself (that’s an understatement).

On our last day in town, Emily called up Tanner, Adam, and me to help move a refrigerator. This wasn’t a refrigerator for your kitchen — it was where the new in-house florist would display their beautiful floral masterpieces. We got it moved and started chatting it up with Ethan, who brought it over. Turns out the dude’s a boss at doing custom work on motorcycles and has turned his business into a lifestyle brand. After having just met us, he invited the three of us to a sweet co-op space where he works.

This is where everything about Nashville really started to make sense for me. Ethan showed us around his work space and then the co-op space in its entirety. People with an array of different skills all work under the same roof: wood workers, motorcycle mechanics, metal workers, and even photographers. Everyone there’s there to do their job and help each other out when needed.

It was really hard to leave Nashville and on our way out we swung by the part of the Cumberland River that runs right by downtown. While I was walking around taking photos of the skyline, listening to the music from the honky tonks across the river, it was evident that there’s more to Nashville than all the lights on Broadway. It’s more than just Music City, it’s a hub for creators.
