Walking to The Wave

06.01.2016/By Adam Fricke/The Road

If you have Internet connection (you're reading this so you do), chances are you've seen The Wave. If not, now you have.

The Wave is a geological phenomenon. Made of Navajo Sandstone dating back to the Jurassic age, erosion has proved that it's pretty crafty. This place is nothing short of a natural work of art.

Since the rock is so fragile, limited people are allowed to access it. The Bureau of Land Management even has a lottery system in place to regulate how many permits are distributed. If you do get a permit, the hike in is your next challenge.

When you win a permit, you're given a map that directs you to The Wave. Essentially, it's a treasure map that leads you to the area. Consisting of 6 outdated photos and vague descriptions, nobody would blame you if you got turned around. The risk in reading it wrong isn't social media shaming, but the fact that you could quite possibly die a lonely death in the Arizona desert. Located in approximately "middle-of-nowhere, AZ" its exact location is kept relatively secretive.

But assuming you don't die amongst the slick rock and sediment, the reward is worth the risk. I've seen some phenomenal photos of The Wave over the years; that's what made me want to visit to begin with. But what photos don't show you is how the pattern in the rock moves as you do. Witnessing the way the light seeps through every nook is worth the treasure hunt. Watching the clouds float through the reflective pool at the base of The Wave is a sight that gets engrained into your memory forever.

If you're not willing to brave the permit process or the treasure hunt from hell, here's a video recapping our afternoon in this desert oasis. It's like you were actually there, right?
